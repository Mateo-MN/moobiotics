Strong from day one.

We're building Calf Start: one sachet that gives a newborn calf's gut the bacteria it needs in its first week of life.

See how it works Get in touch

The problem

The first weeks decide a calf's life.

A newborn calf has almost no gut bacteria. Until the right ones arrive, it is wide open to the infections that cause scours, the biggest single killer of young dairy calves.

56%of unweaned dairy heifer deaths in a US national survey were attributed to scours. Source
RxThe best-evidenced products today are medicines, so farmers need a vet's prescription before they can use one.

Our technology

Colonising the gut early.

Calf Start pairs a proven rehydration base with a gut bacterium no one has brought to animal feed before: Faecalibacterium, isolated from healthy calves.

Faecalibacterium sp.

A butyrate-producing bacterium that supports the gut lining. Calves with more of it in week one grow better and get diarrhoea less often.

Our own calf-derived strain

Lactic acid bacteria

An authorised Enterococcus faecium strain that helps crowd out harmful bacteria.

Electrolyte powder

Sodium, potassium and buffer to keep the calf hydrated, the standard of care on every calf unit.

Oxygen-free packaging

Faecalibacterium dies in air. A sealed, dry compartment with a protective matrix keeps it alive on the shelf until the sachet is opened.

Two doses in the first week. That's all.

No new equipment and no change to the farm routine. The schedule is designed to fit inside the first seven days, when it matters most.

Illustrative timing. The exact schedule will be set by our dose-ranging trial.

1 Keep in the fridge

Each dose is sealed on its own, in packs for year-round and seasonal calving.

2 Mix into warm water

Break the inner seal and empty one sachet into two litres of warm water.

3 Feed the calf

Give it like any electrolyte drink, by teat bucket or bottle.

What the research says

In a published field trial of 554 calves, those given Faecalibacterium in their first two weeks did better than untreated calves.

3.1%vs 6.8%calves with severe diarrhoea
1.5%vs 4.4%deaths linked to diarrhoea
+4.4 kgper calfextra weight gained before weaning

Foditsch et al., PLoS ONE 2015. These results come from earlier research, not from trials of Calf Start, which is still in development.
